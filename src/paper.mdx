<Section id="overview" title="Pose in the camera's own frame">

<div class="copy lead">

MetricPose estimates human joint positions in the camera coordinate frame. Unlike pelvis-relative pose, camera-frame reconstruction must recover body configuration, physical scale, and placement relative to the sensor. These quantities are coupled in monocular images: a smaller person closer to the camera can produce a similar projection to a larger person farther away.

The model combines three complementary signals: learned RGB priors, optional metric depth, and optional ground geometry. A single set of parameters supports RGB, RGB with ground geometry, RGB-D, and RGB-D with ground geometry, allowing metric sensing to be used when available without making it a hard requirement.

</div>

</Section>

<Section id="why" tone="alt" title="Why metric pose?">

<div class="copy">

Root-relative metrics are useful for measuring articulation, but they remove the quantities needed for physical interaction. A robot reaching toward a hand, a system maintaining clearance, and a biomechanics pipeline measuring stride length all need joint locations in a shared metric frame.

**Monocular RGB cannot supply that alone.** A smaller person nearby and a larger person farther away fill the same image rows. Only an extra constraint, such as the floor under the feet or a measured depth, separates them.

</div>

<Section id="distance" title="Controlled distance test">

<div class="copy">

To expose the scale-distance confound directly, we evaluate a small subject across camera heights and prescribed floor distances. The RGB baseline increasingly interprets the subject as a larger person farther away. Ground geometry reduces this error without depth, while RGB-D remains stable across the tested distances.

<Section id="takeaway" tone="alt">

<div class="takeaway">

<p>Metric pose is partly an information problem. RGB priors recover plausible bodies, but optional measurements determine where those bodies actually are. MetricPose separates these roles so a deployment can use calibrated depth or scene geometry when present and still operate with RGB alone.</p>
