## Abstract

MetricPose estimates human joint positions in the camera coordinate frame. Unlike pelvis-relative pose, camera-frame reconstruction must recover body configuration, physical scale, and placement relative to the sensor. These quantities are coupled in monocular images: a smaller person closer to the camera can produce a similar projection to a larger person farther away.

The model combines three complementary signals: learned RGB priors, optional metric depth, and optional ground geometry. A single set of parameters supports RGB, RGB with ground geometry, RGB-D, and RGB-D with ground geometry, allowing metric sensing to be used when available without making it a hard requirement.

</HighlightedSection>

## Why metric pose?

Root-relative metrics are useful for measuring articulation, but they remove the quantities needed for physical interaction. A robot reaching toward a hand, a system maintaining clearance, and a biomechanics pipeline measuring stride length all need joint locations in a shared metric frame.

## One model, three operating modes

| Input | What it contributes |
| :--- | :--- |
| RGB | Learned priors for pose, body shape, and initial camera placement |
| RGB + ground | A floor plane and gravity cue for metric placement |
| RGB-D | Surface depth for scale, camera distance, and local pose refinement |

The model extends a pretrained full-body estimator. RGB features provide the initial reconstruction; depth enters through feature fusion and masked decoder attention; and a second pose pass reads local measured-minus-predicted depth residuals around selected landmarks. Deterministic depth and ground corrections adjust scale and camera translation when their corresponding cues are available.

## Evaluation

We report three complementary metrics in millimeters:

- **Pelvis-frame MPJPE** measures joint error after removing global translation.
- **Pelvis-frame PA-MPJPE** additionally removes global rotation and isotropic scale.
- **Camera-local MPJPE** compares joints directly in camera coordinates, retaining errors in physical scale, lateral position, and camera distance.

On depth-enabled benchmarks, the camera-local metric is the primary measure because it preserves the placement problem the method is designed to solve.

| Method | BEDLAM2 | CMU Panoptic | HuMMan | EgoBody |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| SAM 3D Body | 266.7 | 122.9 | 76.7 | 97.4 |
| SAM 3D Body + depth correction | 184.5 | 102.0 | 52.7 | 70.2 |
| Ours (RGB) | 188.2 | 104.6 | 81.2 | 90.1 |
| Ours (RGB + Ground) | **147.0** | **78.5** | 50.1 | 96.0 |
| Ours (RGB-D) | 166.1 | 98.5 | **39.7** | **47.5** |

These values are camera-local MPJPE in millimeters; lower is better. Ground geometry is especially useful when depth is absent, while measured depth provides the strongest gains on the depth-enabled subjects in HuMMan and EgoBody.

## A controlled distance test

To expose the scale-distance confound directly, we evaluate a small subject across camera heights and prescribed floor distances. The RGB baseline increasingly interprets the subject as a larger person farther away. Ground geometry reduces this error without depth, while RGB-D remains stable across the tested distances.

## Takeaway

Metric pose is partly an information problem. RGB priors recover plausible bodies, but optional measurements determine where those bodies actually are. MetricPose separates these roles so a deployment can use calibrated depth or scene geometry when present and still operate with RGB alone.
